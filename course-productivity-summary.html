Learning outcomes

Six jobs. What every participant can do by 5:30pm on day two

Read

A long report, contract or policy in; the parts that matter, the risks buried in it and the questions worth asking next, out.

Write

Brief AI properly and get a proposal, report or difficult email back at a standard they would put in front of a manager.

Analyse

Take a spreadsheet through to a written review with charts and recommendations, and know which numbers the argument rests on.

Automate

Load their department's context once, teach AI their procedures as reusable Skills, and stop re-explaining themselves every Monday.

Think

Pressure-test a real decision from six perspectives, and argue back when the answer is wrong.

Build

Produce dashboards, calculators and small working applications without writing a line of code.

Underneath all six: knowing what should never go into a chat, how to verify what comes out, and where the limits sit. Taught on day one, before anyone uploads anything.

What they leave with

Day one

Master the conversation

Modules 1–5

"I can get exactly what I need out of AI, every time, in a single conversation."

By 5:30pm every participant can open a blank chat, write a proper brief and finish a deliverable in one sitting — and has felt the friction of re-explaining their job every time, which is deliberate, and is what day two fixes.

01

Introduction to AI and Claude for productivity

70 min

Where generative AI helps a business, what to understand about models, tokens and context, and the Blueprint 6 frame the two days map to.

02

Effective AI communication

110 min

The five ingredients of a brief that works first time — Role, Context, Task, Format, Constraint — practised across three business scenarios and then used on real performance data.

03

AI governance, data security and responsible use

40 min

A classification framework for deciding what information is safe to share, how to spot a data-harvesting tool, and the Responsible AI checklist: verification, bias awareness, human oversight.

04

AI-powered business data analysis: from numbers to insights

65 min

The Analysis Ladder — Prepare, Describe, Diagnose, Prescribe — taken from a raw spreadsheet through to an executive summary with recommendations.

05

Applied AI: individual business problem solving

150 min

An open session where everyone takes a task from their current workload through to a finished deliverable and presents it, closing with an overnight departmental audit.

Day two

Master the system

Modules 6–11

"I never start from scratch again, and neither does my team."

Welcome back and overnight debrief

45 min

Each department shares what its audit found, and the room prioritises the opportunities worth doing first.

06

Projects: building a persistent workspace

75 min

Each participant builds a departmental Project loaded with its KPIs, SOPs and brand guidelines, adds Connectors where they earn their place, and meets Claude Cowork as the shared team surface beside it.

07

Skills: reusable procedures for recurring work

75 min

Moving from ad-hoc outputs to standardised procedures: each participant builds and validates two reusable Skills for their workspace.

08

Artifacts: interactive dashboards and reports

50 min

Building an interactive dashboard as an Artifact, from a first visualisation through to filters, alerts and a KPI scorecard.

09

Decision frameworks: Six Thinking Hats with Claude

45 min

Claude as a debate partner, through Six Thinking Hats, run by the whole room together in Cowork, on a decision each participant is facing now — every claim required to cite a number.

10

No-code application building

80 min

Three working builds through Ideate, Iterate, Output: a task board, a document personalisation tool, and a weekly planner that shows where the week actually goes.

11

Capstone project and strategic deployment planning

70 min

Each participant presents their strongest deliverable, then leaves with a 30-day deployment plan, an ROI figure in hours returned per week, and the Team Cascade Kit for training their own team.
