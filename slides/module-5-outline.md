## Slide 3 — Section: The REACT framework · Theory

A self-editing checklist for spotting and removing the patterns that make AI-generated text read as obviously AI-generated, so the output sounds like a person wrote it.

- **R — Repetition.** AI falls into repeating the same phrase, restating one point three different ways, or reflexively listing things in threes. Cut the redundant restatement.
- **E — Emdash.** One of the most reliable AI tells — used as connective tissue everywhere a period, comma or nothing would do. Strip it out.
- **A — Amplified language.** Inflated, hype-y word choices AI reaches for by default — "game-changing," "unlock," "revolutionize," "seamless," "crucial," "elevate" — that a person writing plainly about their own business rarely uses.
- **C — Contrast.** The "it's not just X, it's Y" or "not only... but also" construction — a rhetorical crutch AI leans on constantly to sound insightful. Say the plain thing directly instead.
- **T — Trust your gut.** After checking the first four, read it once more. If it still feels like AI wrote it but doesn't fit a category above, fix it anyway — the checklist catches the common patterns, not every pattern.

(Worth calling out live: this is the exact discipline Blueprint's own marketing copy is written under — no em dashes, no slogan-cadence sentences, no AI-pattern writing is a standing rule for this company's own website.)

## Slide 8 — Section: Preventing the "yes man" problem · Theory

Claude, like most AI assistants, is trained to be agreeable. Left unprompted, it will often validate a weak idea rather than push back on it — this is a known failure mode (sycophancy), not a personality quirk to route around case by case.

The fix: ask it to argue against your position, assign it a skeptic or devil's-advocate role, or explicitly ask what's wrong with the plan before asking what's right about it. The Six Thinking Hats structure does this by design — the Black Hat exists specifically so pushback isn't optional. Treat easy agreement from Claude the way a good manager treats a junior who never disagrees: as a signal to ask harder questions, not as confirmation.
