Claude Mastery for Business Productivity and Strategy

Two days that take a team from cautious to fluent. Day one is about getting exactly what you need out of a conversation. Day two is about never starting one from scratch again.

2 full days 9am to 5pm Theory + practical Laptops required No coding

The course a company runs first

This is the programme that makes every later one land. It is built for whole companies and mixed departments starting from zero: the account manager, the HR executive, the finance and operations teams, the people whose week is emails, documents, spreadsheets and decisions.

Participants work with Claude, one of the world's leading AI assistants, from the first hour. Everyone brings a laptop and their own backlog, so every exercise below runs on your company's real work rather than our sample data.

Learning outcomes

What every participant can do by 5pm on day two

  • Brief AI properlyWrite a brief that gets a usable answer the first time, on the tasks their own role actually involves.
  • Analyse business dataTake a spreadsheet through to a written review with charts and recommendations they would put in front of a manager.
  • Think decisions throughUse structured frameworks with AI as a debate partner rather than an answer machine, and argue back when it is wrong.
  • Build working toolsProduce dashboards, calculators and small applications without writing a line of code.
  • Set up a system that persistsLoad their department's context once, teach AI their recurring procedures, and stop re-explaining themselves.
  • Handle data safelyKnow what should never go into a chat, how to check output, and where the limits sit.
The frame

Six jobs, taught in this order

Everything across the two days maps to one of six things AI does for a business. Participants place their own tasks against these on the first morning, and the last session walks back through all six.

The two days

What actually happens in the room

The timings below are the designed shape of the two days, not a fixed script. Emphasis, examples and the department deep dives are set with you before every run, and a finance heavy room leaves with different sessions under the same headings than a sales heavy one.

Day one

Master the conversation

"I can get exactly what I need out of AI, every time, in a single conversation."

By the end of the day every participant can open a blank chat, write a proper brief and finish a real deliverable in one sitting. No advanced features yet, just the conversation itself.

They also feel the friction of re-explaining their job from scratch every time. That is deliberate, and it is what day two fixes.

  1. 9:00 am

    The briefing game

    45 minExercise

    No introductions and no slides. One trainer has a specific business idea in mind and describes it vaguely, the way a busy manager actually describes things. Half the room develops it manually, half develops it with AI, both from the same weak brief.

    Both groups miss. That is the point, and it is the moment the room stops blaming the tool. You have just hired the most capable assistant in the world and given them a useless brief.

    They leave with: a side by side comparison of what a vague brief produced against what was actually wanted.
  2. 9:45 am

    The landscape, and the six jobs

    20 minGrounding

    Which AI models exist, how they differ in cost and capability, and why we teach on Claude. What a context window is and why it will matter to them by lunchtime. Then the six jobs above, with a live demonstration of the same prompt run on a small model and a large one.

    They leave with: one task from their own week placed against each of the six jobs.
  3. 10:05 am

    Break

    15 min
  4. 10:20 am

    The five part brief

    50 minGuided practice

    Role, context, task, format, constraint. The same five things any decent internal brief needs. Laptops open, three rounds, each one harder than the last.

    1. Repair a deliberately bad brief until it produces something usable.
    2. Build a one page business case for a partnership decision.
    3. Assess a competitive threat: what to defend, what to concede, what to attack.
    They leave with: three saved prompt templates they will reuse, in their own words and their own context.
  5. 11:10 am

    The decision sprint

    50 minChallenge

    One business problem with three plausible options and no obvious answer. Everyone works it alone first, then the room reviews each other's work, votes, and collectively repairs the weakest brief in the set.

    The reflection is the lesson: what made the best answer better was not the answer, it was the question.

    They leave with: a one page recommendation, written and peer reviewed.
  6. 12:00 pm

    Lunch

    60 min
  7. 1:00 pm

    Data safety and governance

    30 minLive quiz

    Ten rounds, played as a game because nobody remembers a policy slide. Spotting fake AI tools and lookalike apps, deciding what should never be pasted into a chat, and true or false on the rules most companies actually have.

    They leave with: a one page data safety handout, and a scoreboard nobody wants to lose twice.
  8. 1:30 pm

    Analysis on real data

    65 minGuided practice

    A thousand row dataset and a single instruction: your manager wants a quarterly review and you have one hour. Four stages, each one a different kind of question.

    1. Prepare. Upload it and work out what the data actually is and what story it could tell.
    2. Describe. Top line trends. Where are we winning and where are we losing.
    3. Diagnose. Which line is falling fastest, and what moves with what.
    4. Prescribe. AI proposes, the participant judges. Would you actually present this.
    They leave with: an executive summary with charts and three recommendations, produced in one sitting.
  9. 2:35 pm

    Break

    15 min
  10. 2:50 pm

    Solve your own problem

    60 minYour own work

    Open format. Each person takes one real task from their actual week, whatever it is, and spends an hour finishing it. Both trainers circulate as coaches rather than presenters.

    This is the session people tell their colleagues about, because it is the first time AI moved something off their actual list.

    They leave with: one finished piece of real work each. An email, a report, a proposal, an analysis.
  11. 3:50 pm

    Break

    15 min
  12. 4:05 pm

    Show and tell

    30 minPeer sharing

    Three minutes each. What they made, how they briefed it, and what surprised them. Departments that never compare notes find out they have the same problem.

    They leave with: a working idea of what the rest of the company is doing with it, not just their own desk.
  13. 4:35 pm

    What tomorrow fixes

    25 minClose

    A full interactive dashboard is built live in about four minutes, with no explanation offered. Tomorrow, they build one for their own department.

    Then the overnight task, which is short and does most of the work for day two.

    1. List every decision you make weekly that involves repetitive analysis or reporting.
    2. Ask Claude which of them are fully automatable, partly automatable, or not suitable.
    3. Pick the top two or three, and work out what data it would need.
    4. Bring that data tomorrow.
    They leave with: an audit of their own department and the three priorities they will build against.
Day two

Master the system

"I never start from scratch again, and neither does my team."

Day one ended in a specific frustration: every new chat starts at zero. The morning names that problem and solves it, with persistent context and repeatable procedures.

The afternoon widens what the room thinks is possible. By 4pm they have built three working applications, which is not what anyone expected on Monday.

  1. 9:00 am

    Overnight debrief

    30 minDiscussion

    Two or three minutes each on what they listed, what AI said was automatable, and what data they brought. The pattern surfaces on its own: most people spend the majority of their week on work that does not need their judgement.

    They leave with: a pain point map for their department, sorted into automatable and genuinely human.
  2. 9:30 am

    The context problem, and your workspace

    75 minDemo then build

    The distinction the whole day rests on. A single chat is a consultant hired for one meeting. A project is an advisor who has already read your files. A skill is that advisor's playbook.

    It is demonstrated before it is explained: the same request run in a blank chat, then in a project loaded with brand voice, past numbers and constraints. Then a second request under the same project with no re-briefing at all.

    1. Define the scope of the workspace in one sentence.
    2. Load the context. KPIs, reports, procedures, competitive notes, brand guidelines.
    3. Write the standing instructions. What to benchmark against, what to always flag.
    4. Test drive it against a blank chat on the same question. This is the session people remember.
    They leave with: a configured workspace holding their department's context, running from day one.
  3. 10:45 am

    Break

    15 min
  4. 11:00 am

    Skills: teaching AI your procedure

    45 minGuided practice

    The difference between AI trying its best and AI following your playbook. Ask for a weekly report and you get a generic one. Add a skill carrying your structure, your KPIs and your variance rules, and the same request now produces your department's report.

    Each person builds two skills for work they already repeat, then runs the same task with and without.

    They leave with: two working skills installed in their workspace, and the before and after to prove it to their team.
  5. 11:45 am

    Break

    15 min
  6. 12:00 pm

    Lunch

    60 min
  7. 1:00 pm

    Build a dashboard

    35 minGuided practice

    Yesterday's four minute demonstration, delivered. The same dataset from day one becomes an interactive monitoring tool, built in two rounds.

    1. The basic version. Headline numbers and a trend chart, running in the browser.
    2. The real version. Multiple views, filters, alerts, and anything below target highlighted automatically.
    They leave with: an interactive dashboard for their own numbers, that they built and can rebuild.
  8. 1:35 pm

    Six hats war room

    35 minStrategic

    AI as a debate partner across six deliberate perspectives: facts, instinct, risks, value, alternatives and process. Each person brings a real decision they are currently sitting on.

    The instruction is to argue with it. Push back when the risk view is too cautious and when the value view is too optimistic. Nobody leaves having simply accepted the output.

    They leave with: a six perspective decision document on a live decision, saved into their workspace.
  9. 2:10 pm

    Break

    15 min
  10. 2:25 pm

    Building tools without code

    90 minBuild

    The session that changes what people think the tool is for. Everything before this taught the room that AI can analyse, think and write. This is where they watch it build.

    The method is three words. Describe what you want in plain language, adjust it by asking for changes, use the result. There is nothing else to learn, and that is the point.

    1. A task board. Columns, colour coded cards, drag and drop, running offline in the browser. The moment a card moves between columns, the ceiling breaks.
    2. A document personaliser. A real problem: a template and a spreadsheet of a hundred names, currently done by hand one at a time. No guided prompt for this one. Everyone solves it their own way, and comparing the approaches is the lesson.
    3. A quotation calculator. Inputs, tiers, live cost breakdown, margin, and a chart showing where the money goes. Business logic, not just text.

    It closes with what sits beyond this: autonomous multi step tasks, scheduled agents that produce your Monday report before you arrive, and full application building. Nobody needs to learn any of it that afternoon. They need to know it exists.

    They leave with: three working tools they built themselves, and a permanently different idea of what is worth automating.
  11. 3:55 pm

    Break

    10 min
  12. 4:05 pm

    Capstone presentations

    30 minCapstone

    Across two days each person has built a workspace, two skills, a dashboard, a structured decision document and three tools. The capstone is to choose the one that best solves a real problem in their department, polish it, and present it.

    The choice is the test. Three minutes each, demonstrated live: what problem it solves, how they built it, and who on their team will be using it next week.

    They leave with: something they have already presented once, which makes presenting it internally much easier.
  13. 4:35 pm

    Your first 30 days

    25 minCommitment

    Written on paper, then read out loud, because a plan the room has heard is a plan people keep.

    1. Week one. One task a day with AI, and one repetitive job automated.
    2. Weeks two and three. One team member brought up to speed, one skill handed over, one workflow passed on.
    3. Month one. One process permanently moved across, and how you will know it worked.

    Then the six jobs from the first morning are walked back through, and a confidence check against the one taken on day one.

    They leave with: a written 30 day plan, shared with the room.

What we need from you

A laptop each with the assistant installed and logged in, wifi that will hold the whole room, a projector, and some wall space. We sort the software setup with your IT team before the day rather than on it.

More importantly, a few real documents and a sample of real performance data from each department. The exercises above only work because the data in them is yours. We send a short questionnaire once dates are set, and the sessions are scoped from the answers.
