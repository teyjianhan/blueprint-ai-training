Who we are

AI training that people still use after the course ends

Blueprint AI Training was started by two directors who kept seeing the same thing: companies buying AI tools that their people never really used.

The idea is simple to say and surprisingly hard to find. Plenty of courses leave a team feeling inspired for a week. We would rather they leave with habits, built on their own work, that survive contact with a normal Monday.

As an HRD Corp registered training provider, we build every course to be practical rather than theoretical. The gap in most Malaysian companies is not knowledge about AI. It is comfort with it, and comfort is what our workshops are designed to build.

We are not a translated franchise or a licensed curriculum from somewhere else. The courses are designed in Malaysia, around the departments, the deadlines and the way decisions really get made here. The two directors who write the material are the two people who deliver it.

The name is a small promise. A blueprint exists to be built from, and so do our courses.

Blueprint AI Training · Company Profile03

How we deliver

Three formats, one aim

Everything we run is built to end the same way: people reaching for AI on their own, without being told to. What changes is how much of the time is talking and how much is doing.

Fully theory

Briefing

What it is
A talk‑led session with no laptops. What AI is doing to work in Malaysia, what it means for your industry, and what a realistic plan looks like.
Who it suits
Leadership teams deciding whether to invest, or a whole company that needs the same starting picture.
Shape
Short. Works with a large room. Often the step that gets budget approved for the training that follows.

Theory + practical

Workshop

What it is
The default. Each concept is explained, then practised immediately on the participant's own work, so nothing stays abstract.
Who it suits
Teams starting from zero. Most companies begin here, because most people have never been shown how to work with AI properly.
Shape
One to two days, laptops required, capped so every person gets attention. Everyone leaves having finished something real.

Fully practical

Lab

What it is
Almost no lecture. Your team brings live work and we sit with them while they do it, fixing what goes wrong as it goes wrong.
Who it suits
Teams past the basics whose habits have not stuck, or a department with one process they want rebuilt around AI.
Shape
Half‑day sessions, often repeated. The format that turns a good training day into a change you can still see months later.

These stack. A briefing gets leadership aligned, a workshop trains the team, and labs keep it alive afterwards. Each one also stands on its own if that is all you need.

Ground every format covers

Working with AI assistants Documents & writing Analysis & decisions Research & summarising Data safety & guardrails Checking AI's work

The emphasis is set per company. A finance‑heavy team and a sales‑heavy team leave with different sessions under the same headings.

Inside the programmes

What the sessions actually cover

Claude Mastery for Business Productivity and Automation

2 days
  • What AI is, and what it is not
  • First conversations: asking, refining, pushing back
  • Delegating properly: context, examples, standards
  • Your own backlog, finished before day one ends
  • Documents and writing: reports, proposals, minutes
  • Analysis and decisions: long documents, options, briefs
  • Department deep‑dives by role
  • Guardrails, data safety and personal AI workflows

Claude Mastery for AI in Digital Marketing

1 to 2 days
  • Market and competitor research at speed
  • Customer research: segments, objections, language
  • Positioning and messaging you can defend
  • Campaign planning end to end
  • Ad copy and creative variants at volume
  • Reading performance data and deciding what to change
  • Briefing agencies and freelancers with AI
  • Guardrails: brand claims, data safety, checking output

Claude Mastery for Content Marketing

1 to 2 days
  • Teaching Claude your brand voice
  • Content planning and editorial calendars
  • Long‑form drafting: articles, guides, case studies
  • Social posts and scripts from one source piece
  • Repurposing: one asset into a month of content
  • Search and structure without writing for robots
  • Editing and quality control before anything publishes
  • Building a repeatable production workflow
