Claude Mastery for Business Productivity and Automation

Two days that take a team from cautious to fluent. Day one is about getting exactly what you need out of a conversation. Day two is about never starting one from scratch again.

2 full days Day one: 8:30am–6:10pm Day two: 8:30am–5:30pm Theory + practical Laptops required 11 modules No coding

The course a company runs first

This is the programme that makes every later one land. It is built for whole companies and mixed departments starting from zero: the account manager, the HR executive, the finance and operations teams, the people whose week is emails, documents, spreadsheets and decisions.

Participants work with Claude, one of the world's leading AI assistants, from the first hour. Everyone brings a laptop and a task from their own workload. Where your company approves it, the exercises run on your own material; where it does not, we supply realistic mock data and nothing sensitive leaves the room.

Learning outcomes

What your team can do after the two days

One outcome for each of the six jobs, in the order they are taught.

  • ReadPut a long report, contract or policy in and come out with the parts that matter, the risks buried in it, and the questions worth asking next.
  • WriteBrief AI properly, using the five ingredients, and get a proposal, report or difficult email back at a standard they would put in front of a manager.
  • AnalyseTake a spreadsheet through to a written review with charts and recommendations, and know which numbers the argument rests on.
  • AutomateLoad their department's context once, teach AI their recurring procedures as reusable Skills, and stop re-explaining themselves every Monday.
  • ThinkPressure-test a real decision from six perspectives, with AI as a debate partner rather than an answer machine, and argue back when it is wrong.
  • BuildProduce dashboards, calculators and small working applications without writing a line of code.

Underneath all six: knowing what should never go into a chat, how to verify what comes out, and where the limits sit. That is taught on day one, before anyone uploads anything.

The two days

What actually happens in the room

Eleven modules across two days, each one taught and then practised before the room moves on. The timings are the designed shape of the programme rather than a fixed script: emphasis, examples and the departmental exercises are scoped with you before every run.

Day one

Master the conversation

"I can get exactly what I need out of AI, every time, in a single conversation."

Modules 1 to 6. By the end of the day every participant can open a blank chat, write a proper brief and finish a real deliverable in one sitting. No advanced features yet, just the conversation itself.

They also feel the friction of re-explaining their job from scratch every time. That is deliberate, and it is what day two fixes.

Day two

Master the system

"I never start from scratch again, and neither does my team."

Modules 7 to 11. Day one ended in a specific frustration: every new chat starts at zero. The morning names that problem and solves it, with a persistent workspace for one person, then a shared one for the whole room.

The afternoon widens what the room thinks is possible. By late afternoon they have built three working applications, which is not what anyone expected on Monday.

  1. 8:30 am Module 7

    Projects: building a persistent workspace

    75 minGuided practice
    • The limitation of single-chat sessions, and why re-entering context costs so much productivity.
    • Live demonstration: single chat against Project-based output, compared side by side.
    • Building a Claude Project as a strategic command centre for each department.
    • Loading organisational context: KPIs, SOPs, brand guidelines and strategic objectives.
    • Claude's memory feature, and when it is the right tool instead of a Project.
    • Writing custom instructions and configuring Claude Styles for departmental communication.
    • Connectors: reading calendars and company tools with permission, and deciding where that permission should stop.
    • The recurring uses a departmental workspace earns its keep on: meeting preparation, weekly reviews, and turning notes into action items.
    Hands-on: each participant builds and tests a fully configured strategic workspace.
  2. 9:45 am

    Morning tea break

    15 min
  3. 10:00 am Module 8

    Claude Cowork: the shared team workspace

    75 minGuided practice
    • A Project is deliberate and personal; Cowork is the shared version, where multiple people work together in the same session.
    • Live demonstration: the same collaborative task run as separate private chats vs. run together in Cowork.
    • Starting a Cowork session, adding participants, and what each person sees.
    • What belongs in Cowork versus a Project.
    Hands-on: the room re-runs day one's Six Thinking Hats exercise — the same six-perspective decision, but properly together this time, live in Cowork.

    • Building an interactive dashboard from business data, from a first visualisation through to filters, alerts and a KPI scorecard.
    • Iterating on an Artifact: changing structure, adding a metric, correcting a chart without starting again.
    Hands-on: participants build a complete strategic dashboard from the day one dataset.

    Break

  7. 1:20 pm Module 10

    No-code application building

    80 minBuild

    The Ideate, Iterate, Output framework: building functional tools through natural language alone. Three builds, each one a different kind of problem.

    1. Interactive task management board with drag-and-drop functionality.
    2. Automated guest card personalisation, solving a real hotel workflow using a PNG template and guest data integration.
    3. Weekly planner and meeting preparation tool, importing real scheduling data to analyse where the week actually goes, block time against priorities, generate prioritised agendas and produce Monday morning briefings.

    The module closes with an honest map of what sits beyond this course: Claude Code, Claude Design and scheduled automation, what each is for, and when a team is ready for them.

    Hands-on: participants build three working business applications in 80 minutes, without writing code.

On the day: wifi that will hold the whole room, a projector, and some wall space.

If your company is happy to share them, a few working documents and a sample of performance data from each department make the exercises land harder.
